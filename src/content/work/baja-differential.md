## Objective
The goal for the Northwestern Baja Racing team was to design, validate, and manufacture a custom aluminum front differential housing. The primary engineering constraint was balancing extreme durability against severe off-road shock loads while minimizing weight to improve vehicle speed and agility.

## My Role & Contribution
As a lead on the drivetrain and mechanical systems, I owned the end-to-end physical lifecycle of the differential housing:
* Modeling the 3D geometry and managing complex sub-assemblies in **SolidWorks**.
* Researching and computing theoretical load cases and bearing forces on the front differential pinion line.
* Conducting **Finite Element Analysis (FEA)** to simulate stress concentrations and validate structural integrity.
* Generating precise CNC toolpaths using **Siemens NX CAM** for physical machining.

## Engineering Process

### 1. 3D CAD & Assembly Management
I designed the differential housing in SolidWorks, ensuring precise tolerances for bearing presses and gear meshes. Because this part integrates into the larger vehicle drivetrain, I utilized SolidWorks Pack and Go to systematically manage and share complex assembly references across the engineering team without breaking external file linkages.

### 2. Load Calculations & FEA Simulation
Before committing to raw materials, the design had to be mathematically proven. I researched worst-case off-road load scenarios and calculated the maximum bearing forces exerted on the front differential pinion line. I applied these loads in SolidWorks FEA to identify stress concentrations, subsequently removing excess aluminum in low-stress zones to optimize the strength-to-weight ratio.

### 3. CAM & Physical Manufacturing
With the digital model validated, I transitioned the project to manufacturing. I imported the CAD into Siemens NX CAM to generate the multi-axis CNC toolpaths. I carefully planned the machining operations—including facing, pocketing, and contouring—to ensure the physical aluminum billet could be milled efficiently while maintaining the strict tolerances required for the drivetrain internals.

## Results & Impact
* **Validated Structural Integrity:** The FEA simulations successfully proved the housing could withstand worst-case pinion line bearing forces without yielding.
* **Design-to-Manufacture Success:** Successfully bridged the gap between theoretical digital design and physical CNC production, resulting in a lightweight, race-ready aluminum differential housing.
